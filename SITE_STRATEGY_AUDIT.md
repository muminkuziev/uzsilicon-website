# Uz Silicon Tech — Site Strategy Audit

Date: 2026-08-07

## Scope and evidence standard

This audit covers `index.html`, `founder.html`, both Master Plan HTML files, `README.md`, the linked PDFs and `CNAME`. The repository contains no agreements, consent letters, geological reports, permits, financing documents, PPAs, EPC appointments or offtake contracts. Company and institution names in legacy material therefore cannot establish formal status.

## Public-site changes

- Repositioned the homepage from a polysilicon-sales proposition to an integrated silicon-to-wafer platform.
- Defined solar-grade monocrystalline wafers as the primary strategic output and polysilicon predominantly as captive feedstock.
- Labelled up to 100 kt/y and approximately 45–55 GW/y as conceptual and subject to validation.
- Added proposed Turkey and second MENA wafer JVs, explicitly under evaluation.
- Replaced named-partner claims with a role-based consortium architecture.
- Made Gate 1 the principal validation mechanism.

## Unsupported or outdated legacy claims

The previous homepage stated or implied: “Central Asia's first”; fully traceable production; project-owned quartz mining; verified Nurota feedstock; ACWA Power/Masdar delivery of an energy cluster; UzSIF/state equity, land and guarantees; a Turkey wafer partner; India/EU offtake; and fixed CAPEX, margins and dates. No supporting documents for those project-specific claims are present. They were removed or made conditional on the homepage.

The founder page's “working with” wording could imply active institutional relationships. It now describes prospective stakeholder outreach and clarifies that outreach is not a commitment.

## Partner-status risks

- **ACWA Power / Masdar:** Master Plans assign an energy-owner role without repository evidence.
- **UzSIF / State:** Legacy materials assign orchestration, equity, land and guarantee roles without evidence.
- **Turkey wafer partner:** Master Plans assign plant delivery and CAPEX without naming an entity or documenting agreement.
- **Indian / European buyers:** Markets and companies appear in an offtake narrative without an LOI or contract.
- **Universities / public bodies:** Treat as candidates or intended audiences unless engagement is documented.
- **SAC:** No SAC reference was found. If later added, wording must match written evidence, such as “technical discussions” or “preliminary engagement.”

## Master Plan inconsistencies

The Master Plan HTML/PDF files were intentionally not rewritten. They retain:

- 100 kt/y polysilicon as the main production and revenue case;
- 20 GW/y wafer capacity rather than the new indicative 45–55 GW/y range;
- sale of roughly half the polysilicon to India;
- a Turkey partner building a wafer plant in Uzbekistan rather than two proposed international wafer JV platforms;
- ACWA/Masdar and a Turkish partner as assigned asset owners;
- narrow CAPEX, energy, margin, employment and schedule assumptions;
- statements implying feedstock quality is verified;
- specific FID, construction and 2032 production timing;
- state/sovereign commitments not evidenced in this repository.

Because the homepage links to them, these documents may conflict with current positioning. After stakeholder review, visibly version or label them as a legacy conceptual master plan.

## Recommended Master Plan update

1. Rebuild the commercial model around wafers and captive upstream polysilicon.
2. Reconcile material balance with 45–55 GW/y, including format, thickness, kerf and yield sensitivities.
3. Replace assigned company roles with capability packages and selection pathways.
4. Add separate Uzbekistan-upstream, Turkey-JV and second-MENA-JV scenarios.
5. Convert geology, utility, approval, incentive, offtake, CAPEX and schedule claims into evidence-tagged assumptions.
6. Expand Gate 1 into an auditable framework with evidence requirements and pass/fail criteria.
7. Align both languages and regenerate PDFs only after technical, commercial and legal review.

## Human/legal verification required

- Founder biography and intellectual-property wording.
- Geological assays, resource ownership, reserve classification and beneficiation results.
- Any status involving companies, governments, funds, universities or customers.
- Capacity conversion, technology route, yields, utilities and environmental pathway.
- Incentives, customs treatment, market access and JV jurisdiction selection.
- CAPEX, financing, dates, qualification and offtake assumptions.
